# Self Improvement

## Overview

Review recent work for repeated, costly, or error-prone workflows that would benefit from a consistent process. Produce a compact evidence-backed shortlist first, then create only high-confidence missing items.

## Evidence Order

Use available evidence in this order:

- Recent Codex sessions and task summaries.
- Codex memories and rollout summaries for patterns repeated across sessions.
- Chronicle, if enabled, for discovery of repeated work outside Codex. Confirm important details in the relevant source system when possible.
- Existing skills, custom agents, and automations, so the answer reuses or extends what already exists before creating anything new.

## Candidate Criteria

Look broadly across coding, research, writing, planning, communication, operations, analysis, and personal administration.

Only act on a candidate when it:

- Occurred at least twice, or is clearly likely to recur and costly to repeat.
- Has stable inputs, a repeatable procedure, and a clear output or stopping condition.
- Would materially improve speed, quality, consistency, or reliability.
- Is not already adequately covered.

## Choose The Smallest Form

- Skill: reusable workflow or playbook.
- Custom subagent: bounded specialist role or investigation task suitable for delegation.
- Automation: scheduled or recurring check, report, reminder, or monitor.
- Extend existing: existing skill, custom agent, or automation is close enough to improve.
- Skip: work that is too one-off, ambiguous, sensitive, or poorly evidenced to package.

## Workflow

1. Review the available evidence in the required order.
2. Build a compact shortlist before creating anything.
3. For each candidate, record:
   - Repeated workflow.
   - Supporting evidence and dates.
   - Frequency and confidence.
   - Recommended form: skill, subagent, automation, extend existing, or skip.
   - Why it is or is not worth creating.
4. Create only high-confidence missing items. Keep each item narrow, practical, source-aware, and easy to validate.
5. Avoid speculative, overlapping, or overly broad assets.

## Final Response

Finish with:

- What was created or extended.
- What was deliberately skipped.
- What needs more evidence before packaging.
